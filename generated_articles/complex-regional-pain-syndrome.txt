        "Drug selection should be individualized to the dominant barrier—such as inflammatory features, neuropathic pain, sleep disruption, or inability to participate in therapy—and reviewed against functional benefit rather than pain score alone. [13][14]",
        "In early inflammation-predominant disease, oral corticosteroids have supportive randomized-trial review evidence, but the provided literature does not establish a regimen. [1][11]",
        "Bisphosphonates, calcitonin, subanesthetic intravenous ketamine, and free-radical scavengers have been reported as potentially effective in randomized-trial reviews, but treatment selection should be made by clinicians experienced with CRPS and the agent-specific safety profile. Do not infer an FDA indication, standardized dose, or universal sequencing from these data. [1][14]"
